
I'm thinking of adding ranged weapons and ammo to beast road, what do you think?

Yes — I think ranged weapons could be a major upgrade for Beast Road, especially now that you already have loot, gear, enemy variety, exploration, towers, and resource gathering in the design. The key is making ranged combat feel like a scarce tactical option, not turning Beast Road into a twin-stick shooter.

I’d build it around melee as the reliable default, ranged as leverage. Ammo becomes something the player thinks about: “Do I spend these bolts on this elite now, or save them for the next wave?”

A strong starting arsenal would be:

Weapon	Role	Ammo	Identity
Shortbow	Fast general ranged	Arrows	Mobile, cheap, dependable
Longbow	High damage / range	Heavy Arrows	Slow draw, armor penetration
Crossbow	Burst damage	Bolts	Huge single hit, long reload
Repeater Crossbow	Crowd control	Bolts	Faster shots, lower damage
Sling	Early-game ranged	Stones	Nearly free ammo, weak but useful
Throwing Knives	Fast emergency option	Knives	Recoverable from enemies/ground
Javelin	Heavy projectile	Javelins	Piercing, potentially recoverable
Scrap Hand-Cannon	Rare late-game weapon	Powder + Shot	Brutal, loud, expensive
Relic Weapon	Endgame supernatural ranged	Special resource	Build-defining effects
I especially like recoverable ammunition for Beast Road. Arrows, bolts, knives, and javelins could sometimes remain stuck in enemies, walls, or the battlefield after impact. You walk over and reclaim them. That creates a really satisfying scavenger loop without forcing constant crafting.

Ammo could also become part of the existing resource economy:

Wood + Scrap → Arrows
Metal + Wood → Bolts
Stone → Sling Ammo
Metal → Throwing Knives
Sulfur/Charcoal/Salvage → Powder ammunition

Then rarity can affect the weapon, not force you into twenty annoying ammo variants. I would avoid things like “Common Arrow / Rare Arrow / Epic Arrow.” Keep ammunition understandable.

Where it gets especially interesting is elemental ammunition:

🔥 Ember Arrow — burn + ignite environmental objects
❄️ Rime Bolt — slow/freeze buildup
⚡ Arc Bolt — chains between nearby enemies
🌪️ Gale Arrow — knockback
🪨 Shatter Bolt — armor/stagger damage
☠️ Bog-Tipped Arrow — poison
💥 Blast Bolt — tiny AoE
🩸 Barbed Arrow — bleed and increased damage while moving
That ties beautifully into the elemental identity you already have across Beast Road’s tower roster.

One mechanic I’d absolutely add
Give the hero two weapon slots:

Primary: melee
Secondary: ranged

Then one-button weapon swapping.

That preserves Beast Road's close-combat identity while letting moments naturally become:

Slash → dash backward → crossbow shot → enemy charges → swap → melee finisher.

That could make the combat feel dramatically more sophisticated without dramatically increasing complexity.

Ammo scarcity
I wouldn't make ammo brutally scarce.

Something like:

Basic arrows/stones: plentiful
Bolts: moderate
Specialty ammo: uncommon
Firearm ammo: rare
Relic ammunition: extremely rare or rechargeable
Enemies, scavenging nodes, crates, NPC merchants, buildings, and battlefield loot can all feed the system.

Your Scavenging Post and Forge become much more useful too: the Scavenging Post could generate ammo materials, while the Forge manufactures ammunition and improves capacity.

You could even have:

Quiver upgrades
12 → 18 → 24 → 32 arrows

Bolt pouch upgrades
6 → 9 → 12 → 16 bolts

This creates progression without simply making every ranged weapon deal +10% damage.

And ranged enemies should arrive with it
Once the player can shoot, I'd introduce enemies that make the mechanic matter:

Bogkin Archer — basic ranged pressure
Glassborn Spitter — lobbed corrosive projectile
Steppe Javeliner — slow, powerful throws
Warden Marksman — elite sniper-like enemy
Howler variant — suppressive sonic projectile

Suddenly battles aren't just “walk toward the nearest enemy.” The player has targets to prioritize.

And imagine Mirrorfang firing a reflected version of your own projectile back at you. That would be fantastic boss design.

My preferred Beast Road implementation
I would introduce ranged combat with only three weapons initially:

Shortbow → Crossbow → Scrap Hand-Cannon

That's enough to establish three completely different fantasies:

Bow: sustained/mobile
Crossbow: precision/burst
Hand-Cannon: devastating/scarce

Then add throwing weapons, elemental ammo, legendary ranged weapons, etc. after you've proven the core loop is fun.

Most importantly, I would not allow infinite ranged attacks with no tradeoff. Beast Road already has a strong survival/scavenger identity. Ammunition gives you another resource that makes every expedition, kill, chest, and return to the city matter.

I think this would genuinely be one of the better remaining systems you could add — provided we keep its first implementation small enough that it doesn't explode the release scope.

Can and how would players swap ranged weapon and ammo types?

Yes — and I’d make swapping feel fast during combat, but not inventory-heavy.

For Beast Road, I think the cleanest system is:

1 equipped melee weapon
1 equipped ranged weapon
1 active ammo type
Optional 2–3 reserve ammo types assigned to that ranged weapon
Then the player gets two separate actions:

Weapon Swap: instantly toggles between melee and ranged.
Ammo Swap: cycles compatible ammo types for the currently equipped ranged weapon.

So combat might look like:

Sword → Crossbow → Regular Bolt → Frost Bolt → back to Sword

On controller, I’d do something like:

Y / Triangle: swap melee ↔ ranged
D-pad Left/Right: cycle ammo
Hold D-pad Right or a shoulder button: open a small radial ammo wheel
On keyboard:

Q: swap melee ↔ ranged
R or Mouse Wheel click: cycle ammo
Hold R: ammo radial menu
Number keys could optionally select specific ammo directly
On mobile:

A weapon-swap button beside attack
Tap the ammo icon to cycle
Hold the ammo icon for a radial selector
The HUD should always show something like:

Crossbow
8 / 34 Bolts
❄️ Rime Bolt

with the next ammo type appearing faintly beside it, so players immediately understand what happens if they cycle.

I would not make players open the inventory mid-fight just to change ammunition. Inventory should be for equipping which ammo types are available; combat controls should be for switching among them.

A particularly good Beast Road rule would be that each ranged weapon has its own compatible ammo pool:

Weapon	Compatible ammo
Shortbow	Arrows, Fire Arrows, Frost Arrows, Barbed Arrows
Crossbow	Bolts, Piercing Bolts, Arc Bolts, Explosive Bolts
Sling	Stones, Fire Pots, Shatter Stones
Hand-Cannon	Lead Shot, Scatter Shot, Incendiary Shot
Javelin	Standard, Barbed, Explosive, Elemental
That means you never accidentally cycle into ammo your weapon can't use.

I’d also add a “Fallback Ammo” behavior. If your special ammo runs out, the game automatically switches back to basic ammo rather than leaving the weapon unusable.

For example:

You have 3 Frost Bolts.
You fire all 3.
Beast Road automatically switches to your 19 Standard Bolts.

That will feel much better than forcing the player to micromanage it.

And I’d let players customize the order:

Standard → Fire → Frost → Arc

so holding/cycling always follows their preferred sequence.

For Beast Road specifically, my favorite setup is instant melee/ranged swap + tap-to-cycle ammo + hold-for-radial. It gives advanced players speed without making the system intimidating for everyone else.

What else?

A lot, actually — ranged weapons can become a whole new layer of Beast Road without bloating it if we connect them to systems you already have.

I’d expand it in these directions:

Reload behavior by weapon. Bows draw, crossbows reload, hand-cannons have a long reload, throwing weapons recover instantly if another is available. That makes each ranged weapon mechanically distinct instead of just changing damage numbers.
Ammo recovery. Arrows, bolts, knives, and javelins can sometimes be pulled from corpses or recovered from the ground. Higher-quality ammo could have better recovery chances.
Weak-point shots. Certain enemies can expose a head, eye, glowing core, limb, armor gap, or weak backside. Ranged weapons become the best way to exploit these.
Piercing and penetration. Heavy bows/crossbows could hit multiple enemies in a line, while armor-piercing ammo ignores part of enemy defense.
Projectile drop and travel time. Keep basic weapons forgiving, but let long-range weapons reward leading targets and judging distance.
Accuracy states. Moving reduces precision; standing briefly tightens the reticle. This gives ranged combat rhythm without requiring complicated aiming systems.
Quick-shot vs charged-shot. Tap attack for a fast weaker arrow; hold for a stronger, straighter, higher-crit shot.
Perfect-release timing. Release a bow at the ideal point in the draw for bonus velocity or damage. Optional skill expression, not mandatory.
Stagger. Heavy ranged hits can interrupt charging enemies, spellcasters, or elite attacks.
Suppression. Some enemies could hesitate, duck, scatter, or move differently when repeatedly shot.
Noise. Bows are quiet. Crossbows moderate. Hand-cannons very loud. Loud weapons could pull nearby wildlife or enemy patrols toward the fight.
Environmental interactions. Shoot ropes, lanterns, hanging traps, explosive barrels, switches, bells, weak supports, nests, or distant resource nodes.
Hunting. Ranged weapons give wildlife a gameplay purpose beyond ambience. A deer may flee if approached but can be hunted with a bow for food/hide.
Different ammo capacities. Bows use quivers, crossbows bolt cases, firearms ammo pouches. Capacity becomes an upgrade path.
Ammo weight. I would keep this light rather than making every arrow count toward encumbrance. Special ammunition could consume more capacity instead.
Crafting batches. Don't make the player craft one arrow at a time. Something like 1 Wood + 1 Scrap → 8 Arrows.
Field crafting. Basic ammo can be made while exploring; sophisticated ammunition requires the Forge or another city building.
Emergency ammo. If completely empty, certain weapons could have a terrible fallback attack. A bow might fire crude scavenged arrows; a sling can always use stones found outdoors.
Ammo drops tailored to equipment. If you're carrying a crossbow, the loot director slightly increases bolt drops so RNG doesn't ruin the build.
Ammo conservation bonuses. Skills like “20% chance not to consume an arrow” or “recover +1 bolt after killing an elite.”
Ranged-specific affixes. Piercing, Quickdraw, Deadeye, Heavy, Silent, Ricochet, Barbed, Scavenger, etc.
Legendary weapon behaviors. A bow whose third arrow splits into three, a crossbow that chains lightning, a hand-cannon whose kills explode, or a relic launcher that consumes elemental resources.
Weapon mastery. Using bows slowly improves bow-related proficiency; crossbows develop separately. I'd keep this modest so players aren't punished for experimenting.
Ranged skill tree branch. Example paths could be Marksman, Hunter, and Artificer.
Ranged executions. Critically wounded enemies could occasionally become vulnerable to a stylish finishing shot.
Dash-shooting. Certain weapons or perks could allow firing immediately after a dash, creating a very satisfying combat flow.
Melee-ranged combos. This is especially promising. For example: melee launch → arrow shot deals bonus damage; shield break → crossbow crit; ranged stagger → melee finisher.
Enemy projectile deflection. A later skill or relic could let the player parry arrows or even reflect magical projectiles.
Shields. Enemy shield units force the player to flank, use piercing ammo, shoot exposed feet, or swap back to melee.
Ammo-resistant enemies. Not everything should be solved by range. Some enemies should be resistant, evasive, armored, or capable of closing distance quickly.
Ranged elites. Snipers, throwers, artillery creatures, spellcasters, and mounted enemies suddenly become much more meaningful once the player can answer them at range.
Tower synergy. This could be fantastic for Beast Road. Shooting an enemy frozen by a Rime Lance might shatter them; igniting an oil-soaked enemy near an Ember Spire could create an explosion; Arc ammo could interact with Arc Coil effects.
Elemental status combinations. Wet + lightning, frozen + blunt projectile, burning + explosive shot, poisoned + bleed. That could unify player ranged weapons and tower elements beautifully.
Friendly NPC synergy. Hunters, guards, or scavenger NPCs could teach recipes, sell ammunition, recover spent arrows, or improve ranged equipment.
Scavenging events. Abandoned hunter camps, arrow-riddled corpses, military caches, broken siege equipment, or ruined watchtowers become sources of ammo and ranged gear.
Rare ammo shouldn't be hoard-bait. Give players enough opportunities to replenish special ammo that they'll actually use it rather than save 30 explosive bolts forever.
Boss-specific ranged moments. Boss weak points, flying phases, distant summons, interruptible abilities, or projectiles that can be shot out of the air.
And there's one mechanic I think would fit Beast Road especially well:

Ranged weapons as exploration tools
A bow or crossbow shouldn't only deal damage.

You could shoot:

ropes → drop bridges
hanging loot → knock it down
bells → attract enemies somewhere else
lanterns → create fire
animal nests → provoke creatures
distant switches → open shortcuts
traps → trigger them safely
weak walls → reveal secrets

That makes acquiring your first ranged weapon feel like getting a new capability, not merely Sword but from farther away.

I'd probably keep the first release implementation centered on Bow + Crossbow, standard and elemental ammo, weak points, ammo recovery, quick swapping, crafting, and environmental shooting. Then the hand-cannon and more exotic systems can arrive once the core feels excellent.

That would add a surprising amount of depth without changing what Beast Road fundamentally is.

Anything we havent covered?

Yes — there are still a few important areas we haven’t really covered yet, and some of them are probably more important than adding more weapon types.

1. Aiming itself
We talked about swapping and ammo, but not exactly how aiming should feel.

For Beast Road, I’d consider:

Mouse: cursor-based aiming.
Controller: right-stick directional aiming.
Mobile: drag/hold attack toward a direction, with optional aim assist.
Keyboard-only fallback: aim toward movement direction or nearest valid target.
I’d also give controllers/mobile a mild target magnetism, especially against small enemies. Ranged combat feels terrible if players fight the controls more than the enemies.

2. Hip fire vs deliberate aim
You could have two states:

Quick shot
Fast, lower accuracy.

Aim
Hold the ranged attack button to slow slightly, tighten the reticle, and improve damage/crit chance.

That gives players a choice between mobility and precision.

3. Range limits
Every weapon should have a meaningful effective distance.

A shortbow might remain accurate nearby, whereas a longbow gets much more value at distance.

Rather than bullets simply disappearing, damage could fall off beyond the optimal range.

4. Weapon switching should remember state
This is a small UX detail, but important.

If the player has:

Crossbow + Frost Bolts

then switches to sword and comes back later, it should still be:

Crossbow + Frost Bolts

Don't reset them to standard ammunition every time.

5. Reload cancellation
Players should be able to:

Reload → enemy charges → dash/cancel → melee

instead of getting trapped in an animation.

Then perks can modify this:

Quick Hands
Reloading can be interrupted with no penalty.

Steady Hands
Interrupted reload retains partial progress.

6. Loaded ammunition
Crossbows and firearms could remember what is physically loaded.

Example:

You have a Fire Bolt currently loaded, but switch your reserve ammunition to Frost.

The next shot is still Fire.

Afterward, it loads Frost.

That small detail makes weapons feel tangible.

7. Ammo pickup priority
Since Beast Road already has loot magnetization, ammunition should integrate into that system.

If you're carrying:

Crossbow + Bow

your character could prioritize nearby bolts/arrows before irrelevant ammunition.

And when you're full:

BOLT POUCH FULL

instead of silently wasting the pickup.

8. Ammo overflow
Rather than simply deleting excess ammunition:

Option A: Leave it on the ground.

Option B: Convert some into crafting scrap.

Option C: Send it into a temporary expedition reserve.

I'd choose leave it on the ground. It's intuitive and lets players come back.

9. Ranged weapons should affect movement
Not dramatically, but enough to differentiate them.

For example:

Shortbow → normal movement
Longbow → slight slowdown while drawn
Crossbow → full movement, slower reload
Hand-cannon → brief movement penalty during firing
Javelin → movement slows while charging throw
Now weapons feel different even before damage numbers.

10. Recoil and impact
Especially if firearms exist.

A hand-cannon should feel ridiculous compared with a bow:

BOOM → screen shake → smoke → knockback → enemy staggers

Sound/VFX may ultimately matter more than +20 extra damage.

11. Projectile readability
Because Beast Road can already have enemies, towers, effects, loot, and environmental activity, projectiles must remain readable.

Player projectiles should have a distinctive visual language from:

Enemy projectiles
Tower projectiles
Environmental hazards
Friendly NPC attacks
Otherwise battles turn into visual noise.

12. Ammo icons need extremely strong silhouettes
Fire Arrow and Frost Arrow should be recognizable without reading text.

For example:

🔥 red/flame-shaped arrowhead
❄️ crystalline arrowhead
⚡ forked/electrical bolt
🩸 barbed arrowhead

Especially important on mobile.

13. Quick-ammo radial slow motion
If Beast Road is primarily single-player, opening the ammunition radial could briefly slow the world.

Something like 20–30% game speed.

That gives players time to make a tactical decision without actually pausing combat.

14. Favorite ammunition
Players could mark one ammo type as their default.

Then a dedicated command returns to it instantly:

Arc Bolt → Explosive Bolt → Frost Bolt → Default Standard Bolt

Very useful once there are many ammunition types.

15. “Use special ammo only” settings
You could eventually let players configure behavior:

When special ammo reaches 0:

Auto-switch to standard
Auto-switch to next type
Stop firing
Most players choose auto-standard, but advanced players might prefer control.

16. Empty-weapon feedback
Running out of ammunition needs satisfying feedback:

CLICK
OUT OF BOLTS

Then perhaps automatically switch to melee.

That's better than having the player mash attack wondering why nothing is happening.

17. Auto-melee emergency attack
A ranged weapon could have a weak melee attack.

Crossbow:

Bash enemy with stock.

Bow:

Kick/push.

Hand-cannon:

Pistol-whip equivalent.

Not meant to replace the melee weapon — just prevent awkward situations when something gets directly in your face.

18. Ranged weapon durability
We haven't discussed this.

I would not add standard weapon durability unless Beast Road already uses durability elsewhere.

Ammo already provides ranged-resource pressure. Durability + ammunition could become excessive micromanagement.

19. Ammunition shouldn't occupy normal inventory slots
Very important.

Give ammo its own ammunition inventory.

Otherwise players will resent ammunition because every arrow stack competes with valuable loot.

Something like:

🎒 Inventory
🏹 Ammunition
⚙ Materials
🔑 Quest Items

Separate categories.

20. Shops and ammunition economy
NPC merchants could sell basic ammunition, but pricing should prevent infinite effortless restocking.

Standard ammo:
cheap

Elemental ammo:
moderate

Powerful explosive/relic ammo:
expensive or craft-only

This means returning to town involves an interesting decision:

Armor upgrade or another 20 explosive bolts?

21. Ammo recipes as rewards
Instead of finding endless finished ammunition, players can discover:

Blueprint: Ember Arrow

Now one reward permanently expands what the player can craft.

That fits Beast Road's blueprint/resource systems especially well.

22. Enemy ammunition can be loot
Kill an archer?

They might drop:

4–7 arrows

Kill a javelin enemy?

Recover one of their spears.

This creates intuitive loot rather than arbitrary ammo spawning from unrelated enemies.

23. Enemies could run out of ammunition
Potentially very cool, although I'd reserve it for certain enemies.

An enemy archer fires six arrows and then:

draws a knife and charges you.

That makes them feel like actual combatants instead of infinite projectile machines.

24. Ranged enemy telegraphs
Once ranged combat expands, enemy projectiles should have clear wind-ups:

Raise bow → draw → glint → release.

The player sees when to dodge rather than reacting to invisible damage.

25. Projectile interception
Some ranged attacks could collide.

Imagine firing an arrow into:

an enemy fireball

and destroying it.

Or shooting an explosive projectile before it lands.

Could become an advanced skill mechanic.

26. Friendly fire
If Beast Road eventually gets the co-op you've considered, I'd keep player friendly fire OFF by default.

Otherwise explosive ammo especially could become frustrating.

You could have an optional hardcore modifier later.

27. Co-op ammo trading
If multiplayer happens:

Hold ammo pickup → give 10 arrows to teammate.

Or players can drop stacks.

That creates natural specialization:

Player 1: melee tank
Player 2: ranged hunter

28. Loot ownership
Also relevant to multiplayer.

If a rare bow drops, decide whether:

Everyone sees their own loot.
First player grabbing it gets it.
Shared loot rolls.
I'd strongly favor individualized loot for Beast Road co-op.

29. Enemy reactions to ranged combat
Enemies shouldn't simply continue walking forward.

Different AI personalities could:

Rush the shooter.
Seek cover.
Dodge.
Raise shields.
Retreat.
Flank.
Fire back.
Call allies.
This is one of the biggest opportunities ranged weapons create.

30. Cover
Not necessarily a Gears-of-War-style cover system.

Just environmental obstruction:

Rocks
Trees
Ruins
Barricades
Buildings

Projectiles collide with them.

Now terrain suddenly matters during combat.

31. Height advantage
If Beast Road terrain can support it:

Firing downhill could increase range.

Enemies on towers/cliffs become dangerous until the player closes distance.

Even without complicated physics, you can emulate this through bonuses.

32. Weather affecting ranged combat
You already considered weather effects generally.

Ranged weapons give them excellent new applications:

🌧️ Rain
Fire arrows weaker.

🌬️ Heavy wind
Long-distance accuracy slightly reduced.

❄️ Snow
Slower bow draw unless equipped appropriately.

🔥 Extreme heat
Fire effects stronger.

Just don't make weather penalties annoying enough that an entire build becomes unusable.

33. A dedicated Hunter build
The ranged system could support a whole character fantasy:

Hunter

Bow damage
Animal tracking
Arrow recovery
Crit chance
Silent kills
Better hides/meat
Longer detection range
That connects combat, wildlife, scavenging, and exploration.

34. Stealth opener
Even if Beast Road isn't a stealth game, the first undetected ranged attack could get:

AMBUSH x1.5

That makes quietly approaching camps with a bow worthwhile.

35. Weapon inspect / stats screen
A ranged weapon should clearly display:

Damage
Attack speed
Range
Accuracy
Reload/draw speed
Crit chance
Stagger
Ammo type

Avoid twenty stats.

36. DPS isn't enough
A crossbow might technically have lower DPS than a bow but much higher burst.

So tooltips could show characteristics rather than letting players evaluate everything through one number:

Crossbow

★★★★★ Burst
★★★☆☆ Range
★★★★★ Stagger
★☆☆☆☆ Reload

37. Training target in town
This could be excellent.

Put a practice dummy / archery range near the city.

Players can test:

Damage
Range
Ammo
Crits
Weapon switching
without consuming actual ammunition.

38. First ranged weapon introduction
Don't simply put a bow in a random chest.

Give it a little moment.

Maybe the player finds:

an abandoned hunter
his broken camp
tracks leading toward a beast
his bow leaning against the remains

You acquire the bow and immediately get an encounter that teaches how useful range is.

That makes it memorable.

And there's one larger design decision I think we should settle before implementing any of this:

Should Beast Road allow ranged-only builds?
My answer would be yes — eventually, but make them earn it.

Early game:

Melee remains essential.

Mid-game:

Ranged specialization becomes viable.

Late game:

A fully developed Hunter/Marksman build can operate primarily at range, but ammunition economy, fast enemies, shields, and close-range pressure keep it from becoming effortless.

That way we're not merely adding a secondary bow button.

We're adding an entire second combat philosophy while still preserving Beast Road's melee foundation.

Blueprints and crafting, tell me how this should get implemented?

Blueprints and crafting could become one of Beast Road’s strongest progression systems if we make them feel like discoveries that permanently expand what the player is capable of making, instead of just another inventory menu.

Core rule
I’d separate three things clearly:

Resources = what you gather.
Blueprints = what you know how to make.
Crafting stations/buildings = where advanced things can be made.

So if the player finds iron, wood, resin, frost crystal, etc., that does not automatically mean they can craft everything involving those materials.

They need the relevant blueprint first.

For example:

You find Blueprint: Rime Bolts
Permanent unlock acquired.
Recipe added to Forge.
2 Iron + 1 Frost Crystal → 8 Rime Bolts

That discovery should feel exciting because it permanently expands the player’s toolkit.

Blueprints should be permanent knowledge
Once learned, a blueprint should never consume itself.

The player finds:

📜 Blueprint: Heavy Crossbow

They learn it.

From that point forward, every appropriate crafting station knows the Heavy Crossbow recipe.

I would not require the player to keep blueprint items in inventory afterward. Once learned, move them into a Blueprint Codex.

The Codex could show:

Weapons
12 / 34 discovered

Ammo
9 / 28

Armor
7 / 30

Consumables
14 / 22

Structures
11 / 18

Relics / Advanced Crafting
2 / ???

That gives completionists another reason to explore.

How players acquire blueprints
This is where the system gets interesting.

Blueprints should come from different parts of the game rather than one generic loot table.

Source	Typical Blueprint
Exploration	Basic weapons, survival recipes
Elite enemies	Specialized equipment
Bosses	Unique/legendary recipes
Hidden ruins	Ancient/relic technology
NPC quests	Cultural/specialized recipes
Chieftains	Faction-specific equipment
Scavenging	Common utility recipes
Shops	Reliable basic recipes
Events	Unusual temporary-region recipes
City upgrades	Infrastructure recipes
So killing Rust Crown, for example, could permanently unlock some brutal scrap-forged weapon or ammunition rather than merely dropping another randomized sword.

That makes bosses change your future runs.

Duplicate blueprints
Do not let duplicate blueprints become disappointing garbage.

If the player finds a blueprint they already know, automatically convert it into something useful, such as:

Blueprint Fragment

Fragments can buy missing recipes, improve recipes, or research advanced blueprints.

That makes duplicates still valuable.

Recipe tiers
I’d have recipes progress roughly like:

Improvised → Crafted → Refined → Masterwork → Relic

But critically, I would not make these just:

Sword I
Sword II
Sword III
Sword IV

Instead, new recipes should open different gameplay possibilities.

For example:

Hunter Bow
Fast draw, mobile.

War Bow
Slow draw, high penetration.

Bone Bow
Bleed-focused.

Storm Bow
Supports Arc ammunition.

That's much more interesting than simply increasing damage.

Crafting locations
I think Beast Road should have two levels of crafting.

Field crafting
Available almost anywhere.

Keep it limited to simple necessities:

Arrows, basic bolts, bandages, simple consumables, crude traps, emergency repairs.

The player shouldn't have to return to town because they need six arrows.

City crafting
Advanced equipment requires buildings.

Your existing city structure makes this fit extremely naturally.

Forge
Weapons, armor, bolts, metal components.

Scavenging Post
Utility equipment, salvaged components, resource processing.

Sanctum
Relics, magical/elemental crafting.

Granary
Food, hunting products, survival consumables.

Town Hall
Global upgrades and high-tier crafting permissions.

Suddenly upgrading the city isn't merely passive bonuses—it unlocks entire crafting branches.

For example:

Forge Level 1
Shortbow, basic bolts, iron sword

Forge Level 2
Crossbows, reinforced armor, specialized ammunition

Forge Level 3
Masterwork weapons, advanced ammo

Forge Level 4
Legendary construction

That gives city progression enormous purpose.

Crafting should support loot, not replace it
This is important.

If every great weapon is craftable easily, finding loot becomes boring.

If crafted equipment is always bad, crafting becomes pointless.

I’d divide the roles:

Loot

Crafting
Provides control and reliability.

So if you desperately need a crossbow build, crafting guarantees:

“I can make a competent crossbow.”

Some recipes should unlock through discovery chains.

For example:

Discover Basic Crossbow

then later:

Find Reinforced Limbs Blueprint

which unlocks:

Heavy Crossbow

then defeat an elite:

Discover Siege Mechanism

which unlocks:

Siege Crossbow

This gives technology a sense of progression.

Research
I'd consider adding Research later, but keep version one simple.

Duplicate blueprint fragments + resources could eventually allow the city to research unknown recipes.

Example:

5 Blueprint Fragments
100 Scrap
Town Hall Level 3

Research one random undiscovered Rare Weapon Blueprint.

That gives players bad-luck protection.

Ammo crafting specifically
For the ranged system, batch crafting is essential.

Never:

1 Iron → 1 Bolt

Instead:

1 Wood + 1 Scrap → 10 Arrows

1 Wood + 2 Iron → 8 Bolts

8 Bolts + 1 Frost Crystal → 8 Rime Bolts

Special ammo should generally upgrade existing basic ammo rather than requiring completely unrelated ingredients.

That makes the crafting logic intuitive:

Normal Bolt → apply elemental material → Special Bolt

Dismantling
Absolutely add dismantling.

Unwanted equipment becomes materials.

Example:

Iron Sword
→ Scrap
→ small chance for weapon component

Higher-tier equipment might yield:

Fine Metal
Relic Fragment
Blueprint Fragment

This fixes one of the biggest loot-game problems: junk drops becoming meaningless.

Then a subtle:

Added to Forge.

That little moment makes discovery feel significant.

Blueprint rarity
I wouldn't overcomplicate this.

Use something like:

Common
Basic equipment.

Uncommon
Specialized gear.

Rare
Strong build-defining recipes.

Legendary
Boss/secret recipes.

Relic
World-changing/endgame technology.

And unlike random item rarity, blueprint rarity tells the player how significant the unlock is.

A great Beast Road progression loop
This is what I’d want the player experience to become:

Leave city
→ explore Beast Road
→ kill enemies / hunt / scavenge
→ discover resources
→ find a Crossbow Blueprint
→ return home
→ Forge now offers Crossbow
→ gather missing materials
→ craft it
→ later discover Frost Bolt Blueprint
→ now crossbow gains elemental capability
→ upgrade Forge
→ unlock advanced crossbow crafting
→ defeat boss
→ obtain legendary crossbow recipe

Everything feeds everything else.

Exploration → Blueprints → Resources → City → Crafting → Builds → Stronger Exploration

That is the loop I think Beast Road wants.

And for release scope, I’d start with roughly 20–30 meaningful blueprints, not hundreds: a handful of melee weapons, 2–3 ranged weapons, several ammunition types, armor, consumables, and a few city/utility recipes. Once that foundation works, you can expand the blueprint library almost indefinitely without redesigning the system.
