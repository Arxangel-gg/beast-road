# WILDERHOLD — OFFICIAL SKILL SYSTEM v1.0

## Design Goal

Wilderhold's skills should not exist simply to make numbers increase.

A skilled player should gain **new ways of interacting with the world**.

Higher skill levels should:

- unlock new resources, recipes and interactions;
- make difficult environments usable;
- improve efficiency without trivializing resource scarcity;
- interact with climate, natural disasters and Earth's Wrath;
- create cross-skill production chains;
- encourage exploration;
- reward long-term mastery;
- provide persistent progression between runs where appropriate.

The official skill cap is **Level 100**.

Major gameplay unlocks occur primarily every 10 levels. Levels between major milestones gradually improve appropriate values such as efficiency, success chance, yield, quality, durability and action speed.

Avoid large passive stat inflation. A Level 90 player should feel powerful because of **what they know how to do**, not because every action has +900% output.

---

# OFFICIAL SKILLS

1. Woodcutting
2. Mining
3. Fishing
4. Smithing
5. Farming
6. Cooking
7. Engineering
8. Herbalism
9. Archaeology

Systems such as Forestry, Foraging, Construction, Brewing, Composting, Weather Forecasting, Prospecting, Irrigation, Beekeeping, Leatherworking, Surveying and similar mechanics should generally exist as **features or specializations within these skills**, not as separate skills.

Wildlife/Companion progression and Beast Lore remain part of the wildlife/companion system rather than becoming another conventional skill.

---

# UNIVERSAL SKILL RULES

## XP

XP should primarily come from successfully completing meaningful skill actions.

XP reward should consider:

**Base action difficulty × resource rarity × environmental difficulty × quality modifier**

Harvesting something substantially below the player's level should remain useful but eventually award reduced XP.

The system should discourage easily exploitable loops such as:

- repeatedly planting and immediately removing crops;
- repairing deliberately damaged objects;
- repeatedly interacting with the same exhausted resource;
- placing/removing Engineering structures for XP;
- cooking or crafting zero-value recycled outputs indefinitely.

First-time discoveries may award modest bonus XP.

Extremely rare resources and difficult environmental interactions should provide substantial XP without requiring thousands of repetitive actions.

---

# RESOURCE QUALITY

Where appropriate, gathered or produced items may receive quality:

**Poor → Standard → Fine → Exceptional → Masterwork**

Not every item needs quality.

Skill level, material quality, environment and equipment should influence final quality.

Quality should provide useful but controlled differences rather than enormous stat gaps.

---

# CO-OP RULE

Skill XP belongs primarily to the player performing or meaningfully contributing to the action.

For collaborative activities such as Engineering construction or large Archaeology excavations, contributors can receive proportional XP.

Do not globally share all skill XP merely because players are nearby.

Mining extracts meteoric material.

Engineering constructs advanced lightning equipment from it.

Archaeology discovers markings inside the meteor or impact site.

Flood alters soil.

Fishing gains temporary species.

Farming gains nutrient-rich sediment after waters recede.

Engineering drains dangerous areas.

Mining may discover newly exposed deposits.

Archaeology may discover eroded ruins.

## Example: Wildfire

Wildfire destroys forest.

Woodcutting salvages usable burnt timber.

Herbalism finds rare post-fire flora.

Farming eventually benefits from certain ash-enriched soils.

Engineering constructs firebreaks.

Archaeology may discover structures uncovered by vegetation loss.

---

## Example: Earthquake

Earthquake damages infrastructure.

Engineering repairs/reinforces structures.

Mining gains exposed deposits.

Archaeology gains newly uncovered ruins.

Earth-aligned systems/towers may receive temporary benefits.

Every major world system should ideally connect to several skills.

Natural disasters should not merely deal damage.

They should create:

**danger + consequences + opportunities**

A player should occasionally see a disaster and think:

"Wait. What did that expose?"

Skills should interact naturally with Earth's Wrath.

increases Wrath.

causes major Wrath shock.

raises instability.

Engineering:

# THE WILDERHOLD SKILL FANTASY

Early game:

The player takes what the world gives them.

Mid game:

The player learns how the world works.

Late game:

The player understands how systems interact and can deliberately exploit, protect or reshape portions of the environment.

End game:

The player possesses extraordinary knowledge and tools—but Wilderhold itself remains more powerful than the player.

The intended progression is:

**survive the world → understand the world → work with the world → influence the world**

Never:

**level up until the world stops mattering.**

That rule should guide every future skill and environmental feature.

Fletching, Firemaking, Forestry, Foraging, Brewing, Construction, Prospecting, Composting, Irrigation, Meteorology, etc. can all exist—but folding them into these nine makes each skill much richer instead of filling the UI with shallow progression bars.

# WILDERHOLD — PROGRESSION & COMPLETION PACING

## Core Philosophy

Wilderhold should provide meaningful progression for hundreds of hours without requiring RuneScape-scale thousands-of-hours grinding.

Target player experience:

**0–10 hours:** discovery  
**10–30 hours:** establishment  
**30–75 hours:** specialization  
**75–150 hours:** mastery begins  
**150–300 hours:** endgame collection/mastery  
**300+ hours:** completionist territory

Players should regularly unlock something meaningful.

Avoid long periods where the only reward is:

`+1 level`

Instead, progression should continually reveal:

- new tower strategies;
- equipment;
- blueprints;
- companions;
- skill capabilities;
- recipes;
- environmental interactions;
- harder encounters;
- new resource tiers;
- disasters;
- world discoveries.
